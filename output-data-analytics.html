Narrative report

On August 18, 2026, I attended “Multimedia Automation Using AI,” a one-hour webinar organized by Ethel Programming Computer Programming Services and held on Google Meet from 8:30 to 9:30 PM. Ms. Sairine Pregonero delivered the talk to about a hundred participants, and I stayed for the whole session, following the slides and taking notes.

She defined multimedia automation as using AI tools and workflows to reduce manual work in creating and managing media, across three stages: create, edit and publish. AI speeds up production, keeps output consistent through templates and rules, turns one idea into formats for different platforms, and uses analytics to improve the next round of content. She grouped tools by media type, from text and images to audio, video and publishing, and advised asking what task a tool automates instead of chasing tool names, which change quickly.

Best use: automate the boring parts, not the thinking part.

From the slide “Why use AI for multimedia work?”

The idea I’m keeping is that AI should handle the repetitive work while people decide the purpose, the message and the quality. In IT support, the same approach could turn a screen recording of a procedure into a captioned tutorial and a written guide for a knowledge base, or send one maintenance notice to every channel at once, with a person reviewing everything before it goes out.

Key takeaways

  • Multimedia automation spans three stages: create, edit and publish.
  • Pick tools by the task they automate. Tool names change quickly; tasks don’t.
  • AI handles repetition; people still decide the purpose, the message and the quality.
